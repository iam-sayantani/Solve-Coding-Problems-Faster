# How to Solve Coding Problems Faster | 10 Easy Steps
Learning to code can feel overwhelming — especially when you hit your first coding problem. If you come from a non-technical background like I do, even a simple error can shake your confidence. But here’s the good news: problem-solving is a skill anyone can learn with the right mindset and method. In this story, I’m sharing 10 simple steps that helped me think clearly, code better, and solve problems faster — even as a beginner. If you’re just starting out, this is for you.

1. Understand the question :
Take your time to read the problem statement carefully. Don’t rush. Break it down word by word if needed — because often, the key hint lies hidden in the wording itself. Understanding the question deeply is the first and most important step.

2. Link Data Structure/Algorithm
Most coding problems are designed to see if you can associate them with the right data structure or algorithm — even if it’s not always essential for solving the problem. Interviewers often test your practical grasp of DSA concepts. So don’t just study theory — implement it. First, build a broad foundational understanding, then solve problems based on each concept to strengthen your learning. Practice consistently, and avoid getting stuck on one topic — comprehensive exposure matters more than deep mastery of a single area in the beginning.

3. Visualize the Problem
Before writing any code, try to imagine the problem in your head or sketch it out. Use diagrams, flowcharts, or simple drawings to break down the logic. Visualization helps you understand the flow and reduces confusion when you start coding.

4. Start with Easy Problems
Don’t jump into hard problems right away. Begin with easy ones to build your confidence and get used to the problem-solving flow. Easy problems help you get familiar with syntax, logic, and common patterns — like loops, arrays, and conditionals.

5. Remove All Distractions
When solving problems, stay fully focused. Turn off notifications, put your phone on silent, and find a quiet spot. A distraction-free environment will help you concentrate better and think more clearly.

6. Don’t Look at Others’ Solutions Too Soon
Try to solve the problem on your own first. Avoid checking the solution right away — even if you feel stuck. Struggling a bit is part of the learning process. Only check solutions after you’ve given it your best shot.

7. Split the Problem and Solve the Easy Part First
Break the problem into smaller pieces. Identify which parts you can solve easily and start there. Completing even a small part will give you momentum and clarity for tackling the rest.

8. Look at the Simplest Possible Solution
If you’re feeling overwhelmed, step back and think: “What is the easiest way I could do this — even if it’s not efficient?” That basic version is often the first step toward building a better, optimized one.

9. First Brute Force, Then Optimize
Start with a simple brute-force solution that works, even if it’s not the best in terms of performance. Once that’s working, you can improve it step by step. Don’t try to write the perfect solution in the first attempt.

10. Understand Input-Output Mapping
Before writing code, understand how inputs are transformed into outputs. Go through examples manually. Write them out. This will help you notice patterns and build logic based on real cases.


Final Tip: Practice regularly. Coding is a skill that improves with consistency, not speed. Even 1 problem a day will build your confidence and skillset over time.
